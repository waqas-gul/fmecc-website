## 2. Content rules for stock media

- Stock media is **illustrative service photography only**. It must never be captioned, described or placed so that it reads as FMECC's completed work, clients, sites, staff or equipment.
- Stock is never used on the Projects page or in project entries. Projects stay empty until FMECC supplies verified projects (MASTER_PLAN §6).
- Alt text describes what the image shows ("Communications tower with panel antennas against a clear sky"), never "our tower", "our team" or a location claim.
- No asset that shows a named, recognisable real project or another contractor's own installation (for example a company posting photos of its own jobs). See the rejected list in §12.
- The procedural 3D scenes, their posters and their illustration labels stay exactly as they are. Stock images fill the `hero` (16:9) and `showcaseMedia` (4:5) slots only. On service pages the showcase remains the 3D scene or the coverage diagram.
- Pexels, Unsplash and Pixabay forbid implying endorsement by people or brands in the image, and forbid showing identifiable people in a bad light. The selected images show people at work, which is fine. Cameras, cranes or cabinets with readable brand names must be avoided or retouched out (the licences allow modification).

## 4. How candidates were verified

- Every listed asset's own page was opened on 2026-10-28 and its title, creator, licence label and size read from that page. No URL was guessed. Nothing was downloaded, so **no pixel has been inspected yet.** Grade, logos, faces, watermarks and composition are judged from the provider's title, description, tags and listed colours, and need a visual check before approval (open each link).
- Primaries and key backups marked **re-verified** were opened a second time by the lead reviewer to confirm the page, size and licence.
- Size sources:
  - **(page)**: shown on the provider's page.
  - **(Unsplash API)**: Unsplash photo pages don't show pixel size, so it was read from Unsplash's own photo metadata endpoint.
  - **est.**: an estimate, always labelled.
- Colour notes ("listed colours") are the dominant colours the provider shows. They hint at the grade but are not a substitute for looking.

Destination paths follow the pipeline (DESIGN §12.2, ARCHITECTURE §11): originals go to `media-src/<path>.jpg`, `npm run images` writes `public/images/<path>-<w>.webp`, and components reference `/images/<path>.webp`.

## 11. Download, processing and performance

The pipeline exists (`npm run images`, sharp) and ffmpeg 8.1.1 is installed on the workstation, so integration needs no new packages.

**Images**
1. Download each approved original at full size from its source page. iStock: purchase under an account authorised to bind FMECC, and keep the invoice.
2. Crop to the slot's ratio before saving into `media-src/`; `npm run images` resizes by width only and does not crop. Save the crop at:
   - 2560×1440 for 16:9 heroes;
   - 1600×2000 for 4:5 showcases;
   - 2400×1600 for 3:2 gallery images.

   Use JPEG quality 90. That keeps `media-src/` small, est. 0.5–1.5 MB per file, instead of 5–20 MB originals.
3. Optional consistency pass: a light, uniform cool grade with slight desaturation, applied the same way to every image (DESIGN §12.1). All licences allow modification. This needs design approval before it is applied.
4. `npm run images` writes 320–1920 w WebP at q75. Budgets (MASTER_PLAN §8):
   - hero 1920 w ≤ 250 KB;
   - card or showcase sizes ≤ 80 KB at the widths cards request.

   Estimate: dark, low-detail frames such as the night poster and the control room land well under budget; detailed daylight sites may need q70 at 1920 w. Measure after generation.
5. Only each page's hero uses `preload`; showcases and galleries lazy-load (already how `MediaImage` is wired).

**Hero video**
1. Download the UHD (3840×2160) rendition. Estimated download for 19 s: 30–80 MB. Don't use an 8K rendition.
2. Trim a calm 10 s segment (DESIGN: 8–12 s), strip audio, scale to 1920×1080, keep 25 fps. If the motion is continuous, make the loop seamless with a short crossfade between the end and the start.
3. Encode both formats:
   - H.264 MP4, CRF about 28–32, `-movflags +faststart`, target ≤ 3 MB;
   - VP9 WebM, target ≤ 2 MB.

   Estimate: a calm night clip at 1080p for 10 s fits both budgets. Verify the output size, and raise the CRF if needed.
4. Extract frame 0 of the trimmed loop as `media-src/home/home-hero-poster.jpg` at 2560×1440. It becomes the LCP WebP.
5. Keep the 4K master outside git: it can be downloaded again from its page. `media-src/` holds stills only.

**Estimated totals for the recommended set** (est.): about 120–250 MB of downloads (one 4K clip and ten full-size stills). After processing, about 10–14 MB in `media-src/` and about 4–6 MB of WebP plus 5 MB of video in `public/`. Pages load only the widths they need.

**Integration touch points (after approval, not now)**
- `src/widgets/home-hero/data/content.ts`: real poster asset; `placeholder` removed from `heroVideo`.
- `src/entities/service/data/<slug>.ts` (×4): `hero` and `showcaseMedia` become real `ImageAsset`s (width, height and alt from the final crops).
- `src/app/about/page.tsx`: `heroMedia`.

### 16.3 Hero video
- **Source:** a single continuous shot with a slow downward camera move; no cuts or flicker (frame-difference and scene checks).
- **Loop:** a one-way move cannot loop seamlessly, and a crossfade would double the bright crane lights. The loop is therefore 6 s forward plus the same frames reversed, without repeating the end frames. The step from the last frame to the first matches the normal frame-to-frame change.
- **Encodes:**
  - MP4 (H.264 High, CRF 23, faststart, no audio): **1.92 MB** (budget ≤ 3 MB).
  - WebM (VP9, CRF 36, no audio): **265 KB** (budget ≤ 2 MB).
  - Both 1920×1080 at 25 fps, 298 frames, 11.92 s.
  - Against the source frame: SSIM 0.945 / 0.947, PSNR 40.4 / 40.0 dB, with no visible banding.
- **Poster:** the loop's first frame, matching video frame 0 to within encoding noise. WebP 320–1920 px is 2–35 KB.
- **Playback:** from 1024 px only (`VideoBackground` `desktopOnly`). Below that, and with reduced motion or Save-Data, the poster stands alone and no video file is requested. On desktop, reduced motion and Save-Data still offer the Play control.
